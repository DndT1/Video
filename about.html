About the Project

Motivation and Purpose

In the modern gaming landscape, video games have evolved into increasingly expansive and time-demanding experiences. However, while game worlds continue to grow in scale, players often face limited leisure time. Many high-profile titles demand anywhere from 40 to over 100 hours to complete—a substantial commitment that consumers frequently underestimate before purchasing.

This project was created to bring clarity and transparency to video game completion times. By analyzing crowdsourced playtime metrics from HowLongToBeat, this site provides players with clear, empirical expectations about the time investment required for different titles and playstyles.

First Impressions and Expectations

Video game cover art serves as the initial bridge between developers and players. The collage above illustrates how packaging conveys a game’s identity—from vibrant, stylized art to gritty, cinematic action scenes. These visual cues are carefully designed to capture attention, establish atmosphere, and communicate what players should anticipate.

Yet, while cover art effectively communicates tone and genre, it rarely reveals the scale of time demanded inside. Players drawn in by compelling artwork often commit to purchases without knowing whether a game is a focused weekend playthrough or a multi-month journey.

Quality Over Length

Data across review platforms like Metacritic demonstrates that critical acclaim is not tied to campaign length. Compact, narrative-focused 5-hour indie titles frequently share top ratings alongside sprawling 80-hour open-world epics. Ultimately, what matters most is execution and quality, not simply hour counts. This project aims to help gamers find titles that best match their personal schedules and play preferences.
